5 charges to look for on every hospital bill

First, request an itemized bill — the summary they mail you hides everything. Then check these five things, which quietly inflate a huge share of medical bills.

1. Duplicate charges

The same procedure code or supply billed twice. It happens constantly with imaging, labs, and medications when systems double-post. Scan for identical code + amount pairs.

2. Upcoded visit levels

Office and ER visits are billed at "levels" of complexity (e.g., 99285 is the highest ER level). If a routine visit is billed at the top level, the documentation has to support it. If it doesn't, that's a downcode you can request.

3. Unbundled labs and panels

Some tests are supposed to be billed together as one panel. "Unbundling" charges each component separately at a higher total. A metabolic panel billed as ten line items is a red flag.

4. Phantom charges for supplies and time

Items you never received, more "units" than possible, or room/time charges that don't match your actual stay. Compare the bill against what you remember and against your dates of service.

5. Above-benchmark prices

Even correct charges can be wildly above the typical rate for your area. Comparing against benchmark prices gives you leverage to negotiate down — especially if you're uninsured or paying out of pocket.

Then dispute it in writing

List each questioned line, ask for a corrected itemized statement, and request that collections pause while it's reviewed. Under the No Surprises Act, uninsured/self-pay patients also have dispute rights when a bill exceeds a good-faith estimate by more than $400.
