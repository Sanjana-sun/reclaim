Denied a GLP-1 for "step therapy"? Do this.

Step therapy means the plan wants you to try (and fail) a cheaper drug first. It's one of the most appealable denials — because exceptions are built into the rules.

What "step therapy" actually means

Your plan isn't saying the medication won't work — it's saying it won't pay until you've stepped through a required alternative. The key: nearly every step-therapy protocol has a medical exception process, and that's your opening.

Grounds for an exception

An exception is typically warranted when any of these are true and documented by your prescriber:

  • You've already tried the required drug and it failed or stopped working.
  • You had an adverse reaction or can't tolerate it.
  • It's contraindicated or expected to be ineffective for your specific condition.
  • You're stable on your current therapy and switching risks harm.

What to put in the appeal

The winning appeal leans on your prescriber's clinical judgment — not yours. Include a letter of medical necessity that states the exception basis, documents any prior drug trials and their outcomes/dates, and references relevant clinical guidelines. The plan should evaluate the exception on those merits.

Watch the deadline

For non-urgent requests you generally have up to 180 days on commercial/ACA plans, but there are also expedited paths when a delay would jeopardize your health — ask for one if that applies.
